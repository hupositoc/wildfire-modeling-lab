# Research case study

## The original question

The master's research investigated the progression of an existing wildfire using environmental variables and deep Q-learning. It was not a marine-certification tool. Its spatial grid and neighboring actions provided a way to express hypotheses about fuel, moisture, wind and terrain as a learned spread policy.

The practical research output was a simulated footprint that could be compared with a burned-area map. This makes the work more specific than simply “using AI”: the engineer must define state/action representation, spatial units, reward, training dynamics and the meaning of an evaluation score.

## What the thesis reports

The historical document describes a 50×50 Uljin case, environmental preprocessing, DQN learning, reward-weight search and comparison with a damage footprint. It reports Jaccard 0.726 for one weight combination and discusses a Uiseong–Andong application. That is a thesis-reported result. The available MATLAB scripts do not constitute the recovered final implementation, and the value is not presented as a newly reproduced measurement.

## Why the engineering follow-up matters

A numerical result is reproducible only when another engineer can inspect how it was obtained. Reviewing the thesis exposed several barriers: inconsistent NDVI scaling, a floor/ceiling conflict, a time-endpoint/count mismatch, missing mask-extraction settings and unspecified training/branching choices. A file named actual_fire_data was generated by a simulation, so its name could not justify treating it as observational truth.

The follow-up therefore reconstructs the specified parts and makes the unspecified parts explicit. It adds unit-tested units/coordinates/transitions, a real replay-buffer and target-network training loop, deterministic seeds, saved checkpoints, raw experiment traces, a data-source helper and a provenance-gated observed-mask evaluator. These are implemented mechanisms, not a promise that the final score will match.

## What could be done with the implementation

The new code can run controlled synthetic experiments, compare different readings of the printed vegetation equation, inspect how policies respond to reward design and calculate a spatial score when a valid observed mask is supplied. It can also train on a reconstructed observational environmental tensor with an explicit provenance record. The checkpoint is frozen before target-mask evaluation.

The six recorded synthetic runs demonstrate that the training loop executes and produces inspectable results. They also show why reward cannot be used as a substitute for spatial accuracy. An agent may collect favorable repeated rewards without modeling a useful fire front. Identifying this distinction is part of the research result.

## Contribution and remaining work

The author's original contribution is the thesis research problem, environmental-reward model and historical experiments described in the supplied document. The current follow-up is an AI-assisted engineering reconstruction and audit of those claims. Neither the assistant nor the documentation invents a completed real-event reproduction, exclusive authorship of dependencies or an operational wildfire service.

The next meaningful milestone is to recover the final experiment inputs/code and resolve the listed ambiguities, then publish the actual Uljin overlap and a frozen cross-event evaluation. A lower score or non-reproduction is a valid recorded outcome. A target score is not a reason to alter the evaluation dataset.
